REMINDER: This SIEM rule is susceptible to False Positives. Palo Alto Threat logs (Vulnerability/IPS signatures) frequently trigger on poorly formed but legitimate protocol traffic, noisy applications, or threats that the firewall has already successfully blocked. Your primary task is to critically analyze the logs to filter out these False Positives.
Analyze the provided Local-to-Remote (L2R) threat logs, paying close attention to the 'Threat_Name', 'App', and 'PA_Action' fields. Apply the following heuristics to map your findings to the scoring rubric:
1. Map to 0.0 - 0.3 (CLEAR FALSE POSITIVE) if the 'PA_Action' is 'Drop', 'Reset', or 'Block' (meaning the threat was successfully contained and poses no risk), OR if the 'App' is a known noisy/legitimate protocol (e.g., 'bittorrent', 'skype', 'stun') and the 'Threat_Name' indicates a generic protocol anomaly (e.g., 'Malformed Packet', 'Info Leak'). Verdict: 'False_Positive_or_Contained'.
2. Map to 0.4 - 0.6 (SUSPICIOUS BUT INCONCLUSIVE) if the 'PA_Action' is 'Allow', but the 'Threat_Name' does not explicitly indicate known malware. For example, generic exploit attempts, brute force on unusual external ports, or unrecognized outbound activity. Verdict: 'Suspicious_Outbound_Activity'.
3. Map to 0.9 - 1.0 (CONFIRMED COMPROMISE) if the 'PA_Action' is 'Allow' AND the 'Threat_Name' explicitly identifies Spyware, Command-and-Control (C2), Trojans, or Exploit Kits (e.g., 'Zeus', 'Locky', 'Cobalt Strike'). This confirms that strictly malicious traffic successfully bypassed the firewall from an actively infected internal host. Verdict: 'Active_Malware_L2R'.
